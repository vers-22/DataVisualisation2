Organ donation in Australia · 2025

One conversation stands between 2,000 Australians and a transplant

Last year 557 people became organ donors after they died. Their gift reached 1,438 people. Most Australians say they support donation, yet only about one in three has registered, and half of grieving families still say no.

557 deceased organ donors in 2025
1,438 people who received a transplant
20.2 donors per million people, against a national target of 25

Each dot is one person. One donor can save or transform the lives of up to seven people, which is why the grey block is so much bigger.

Part 1 · The odds

Almost nobody dies in a way that makes donation possible

Organs can only be donated if someone dies in an intensive care unit, usually on a ventilator. Fewer than 2 in every 100 hospital deaths happen this way, so every one of those chances matters.

Source: Organ and Tissue Authority, 2025 donation and transplantation data.

A national reform worked, then stalled

For twenty years Australia hovered around 200 donors a year. In 2009 the Commonwealth set up the Organ and Tissue Authority and the DonateLife network of specialist hospital staff. Donor numbers climbed almost every year after that, peaking at 554 in 2018.

COVID-19 hit hard: intensive care units filled and donation fell to 421 donors in 2021. Numbers have now recovered to a record 557, but the rate per person is still below its 2018 level.

Source: ANZOD Registry Annual Report 2026, Appendix I, Tables A1.1 and A1.24.

Who becomes a donor?

Because donation depends on dying in intensive care, donors are mostly people aged 35 to 74 who have had a stroke, a brain injury or a cardiac arrest. Three in five donors since 2021 have been men.

Young children are rare donors, but every age group contributes, including people aged 75 and older.

Source: ANZOD Registry Annual Report 2026, Appendix I, Table A1.2.

Part 2 · The conversation

The bottleneck is one conversation

When donation is possible, a specialist asks the family. In 2025 only 53% said yes, down from a peak of 60% in 2018. Of the 1,500 families asked, about 700 said no.

The single biggest thing that changes the answer is whether the person who died had registered on the Australian Organ Donor Register. When they had, about 8 in 10 families agree. When they had not, it falls to about 4 in 10. Families who know what their loved one wanted find the decision much easier.

Yet only around 8 million Australians, about 35% of people eligible, have registered, even though 4 in 5 say they support donation.

Source: Organ and Tissue Authority, 2025 donation and transplantation data.

Part 3 · The map

Where you live changes the odds

Donation rates are measured as donors per million people, so large and small states can be compared fairly.

Tasmania is the stand-out: 25 donors from about 575,000 people gives 43.4 per million. In 2024 only Spain and the United States reported higher national rates.

Every other state and territory sits below the national target of 25 per million. Victoria is closest at 22.1.

Small states swing a lot from year to year: one or two extra donors in the Northern Territory moves its rate by four to eight points.

Sources: ANZOD Registry Annual Report 2026, Table 2.1; ABS state boundaries (ASGS Edition 3).

Five of the eight states and territories donate at a lower rate than in 2018, matching the national fall in family consent. The ACT and Victoria fell furthest.

Read across a row to follow one state over time. The slow darkening from left to right is the 2009 reform at work; the lighter cells in 2020–2021 are the pandemic.

Source for both charts: ANZOD Registry Annual Report 2026, Table 2.1. Rates use June 2025 ABS population.

Part 4 · The journey

From hospital bed to operating theatre

Donation happens in intensive care units across 120 hospitals, but the busiest 15 provide half of all donors. The organs then often travel interstate to the patient who is the best match.

Each hexagon is a state or territory, drawn the same size so small places are not lost. Click a hexagon to show only the organs donated in that state; double-click to show all again. In 2025 no organs were transplanted in Tasmania, the Northern Territory or the ACT, so all of theirs were flown interstate. 52 of Tasmania's 73 organs went to Victoria.

Sources: ANZOD Registry Annual Report 2026, Appendix I, Table A1.25; ABS state boundaries.

Large hospitals with major trauma, neurosurgery and intensive care units see the most donors. The Royal Melbourne and The Alfred together had 305 donors between 2021 and 2025.

Regional hospitals matter too. Newcastle's John Hunter Hospital had 30 donors in 2025, the third most in the country, and the Gold Coast and Townsville university hospitals are among the 15 busiest.

Part 5 · The world

Other countries are further ahead

In 2024 Spain recorded 53.9 donors per million people and the United States 49.7. Australia's 19.7 placed it 21st of the 83 countries that reported, behind Portugal, Italy, France and Canada, and about a third of Spain's rate.

Every country on this map uses IRODaT's 2024 figures, so all are measured the same way in the same year. That is why Australia shows 19.7 here but 20.2 elsewhere on this page, which is ANZOD's 2025 figure. Malta (12.0), Singapore (4.8) and Hong Kong (4.1) also reported but are too small to see at this scale. Canada's figure counts utilised donors only.

Source: International Registry in Organ Donation and Transplantation (IRODaT), Worldwide Actual Deceased Organ Donors Rate 2024 (pmp).

Registering takes one minute

Register at donatelife.gov.au, then tell your family. It is the one thing that most changes what they will say if they are ever asked.
