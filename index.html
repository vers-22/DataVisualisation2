Organ Donation in Australia

Last year 557 people became organ donors after they died. Their gift reached 1,438 people. Most Australians support donation, yet only about one in three has registered, and half of grieving families still say no.

557 deceased organ donors in 2025
1,438 people who received a transplant
20.2 donors per million people, against a national target of 25

Each dot is one person. One donor can save or transform up to seven lives, so the grey block is much bigger.

Part 1 · The odds

Almost nobody dies in a way that makes donation possible

Organs can only be donated if someone dies in intensive care, usually on a ventilator. Fewer than 2 in 100 hospital deaths happen this way, so every chance matters.

Source: Organ and Tissue Authority, 2025 donation and transplantation data.

A national reform worked, then stalled

For twenty years Australia had about 200 donors a year. In 2009 the Commonwealth set up the Organ and Tissue Authority and the DonateLife network of hospital specialists. Donor numbers then climbed almost every year, peaking at 554 in 2018.

COVID-19 hit hard: intensive care units filled, and donors fell to 421 in 2021. Numbers have recovered to a record 557, but the rate per person is still below 2018.

Source: ANZOD Registry Annual Report 2026, Appendix I, Tables A1.1 and A1.24.

Who becomes a donor?

Donation depends on dying in intensive care, so most donors are between 35 and 74 years old, following a stroke, brain injury or cardiac arrest. Three in five donors since 2021 were men.

Young children are rare donors, but every age group contributes, including people aged 75 and older.

Source: ANZOD Registry Annual Report 2026, Appendix I, Table A1.2.

Part 2 · The conversation

The bottleneck is one conversation

When donation is possible, a specialist asks the family. In 2025 only 53% said yes, down from 60% in 2018. Of 1,500 families asked, about 700 said no.

The biggest thing that changes the answer is whether the person had joined the Australian Organ Donor Register. If they had, about 8 in 10 families agree. Otherwise, agreement falls to about 4 in 10. Knowing a loved one's wishes makes the decision easier.

Yet only about 8 million Australians, around 35% of those eligible, have registered, though 4 in 5 support donation.

Source: Organ and Tissue Authority, 2025 donation and transplantation data.

Part 3 · The map

Where you live changes the odds

Donation rates are measured as donors per million people, so large and small states can be compared fairly.

Tasmania stands out: 25 donors from about 575,000 people gives 43.4 per million. In 2024 only Spain and the United States had higher national rates.

Every other state and territory is below the national target of 25 per million. Victoria is closest, at 22.1.

Rates in small states swing widely from year to year. One or two extra donors shifts the Northern Territory's rate by four to eight points.

Sources: ANZOD Registry Annual Report 2026, Table 2.1; ABS state boundaries (ASGS Edition 3).

Five of the eight states and territories donate at a lower rate than in 2018, matching the national fall in family consent. The ACT and Victoria fell furthest.

Part 4 · The journey

From hospital bed to operating theatre

Donation happens in intensive care units across 120 hospitals, but the busiest 15 provide half of all donors. The organs then often travel interstate to the best-matched patient.

Each hexagon is a state or territory, all drawn the same size so small places are not lost. Click a hexagon to show only that state's organs; double-click to show all again.

In 2025 no organs were transplanted in Tasmania, the Northern Territory or the ACT, so all of theirs were flown interstate. 52 of Tasmania's 73 organs went to Victoria. Sources: ANZOD Registry Annual Report 2026, Appendix I, Table A1.25; ABS state boundaries.

Large hospitals with major trauma, neurosurgery and intensive care units see the most donors. The Royal Melbourne and The Alfred together had 305 donors from 2021 to 2025.

Regional hospitals matter too. Newcastle's John Hunter Hospital had 30 donors in 2025, the third most in Australia. The Gold Coast and Townsville hospitals are in the busiest 15.

Part 5 · The world

Other countries are further ahead

In 2024 Spain recorded 53.9 donors per million people and the United States 49.7. Australia's 19.7 placed it 21st of 83 countries, about a third of Spain's rate.

Every country here uses IRODaT's 2024 figures, so all are measured the same way in the same year. That is why Australia shows 19.7 here but 20.2 elsewhere on this page (ANZOD, 2025).

Malta (12.0), Singapore (4.8) and Hong Kong (4.1) also reported but are too small to see. Canada's figure counts utilised donors only. Source: International Registry in Organ Donation and Transplantation (IRODaT), Worldwide Actual Deceased Organ Donors Rate 2024 (pmp).
